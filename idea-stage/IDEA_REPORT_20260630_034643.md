## Executive Recommendation

The strongest paper path is not "add scaffold" or "add flow." The defensible ADAGS story is:

1. Make dynamic-region diagnostics the claim surface: sharpness/detail, static ghosting, track/flow reliability, and fixed realized budget.
2. Lead with reliability-gated rendered flow or confidence-gated priors as the method contribution.
3. Use reversible LoRA route0 as the stable base and reserve residual/scaffold/part capacity for diagnosed failures.

Top 3:

1. **Reliability-Gated Rendered Flow For Dynamic Cores** - best current W&B signal among method ideas.
2. **Dynamic-Region Diagnostic Benchmark As The Paper Backbone** - highest feasibility and necessary for any credible claim.
3. **Route0-Coherent Plus Residual-Specialist Motion** - strongest higher-novelty method if diagnostics show route0 failure is localized.

Eliminate as paper leads: unanchored scaffold residuals, naive broad flow loss, hard static conversion, and blur/early part-basis variants as previously tried.

## Landscape Summary

ADAGS is a per-scene dynamic Gaussian Splatting project focused on N3V cooking scenes, where the visible failure is fast moving food, flames, hands, and heads becoming smeared while static regions can still score well. The stable empirical base is reversible LoRA route0. W&B fixed-budget and mechanism-screen results repeatedly make plain route0 stronger than broad scaffold or dynamic-densification variants in global PSNR.

The current branch already has useful hooks: `MotionPriorCache`, dynamic ROI loss, static exclusion loss, rendered-flow and track-flow supervision paths, scaffold residual motion, motion-aware densification, and dynamic/static diagnostics. However, the most important knobs are often inactive in configs: `lambda_track_flow: 0.0` and `enable_rendered_flow: false` appear across many shipped configs. That means existing negative scaffold results are mostly evidence against unanchored scaffold, not against correspondence-anchored scaffold.

The literature pressure is severe. MoSca and Prior-Enhanced GS crowd plain scaffold claims; MotionGS, SplatFlow, PaMoSplat, and Flow4DGS-SLAM crowd plain flow claims; RiGS, MoE-GS, MAPo, PaMoSplat, HiCoM, and continuous-motion work crowd generic motion specialization; AdaGaR and Multi4D make high-frequency dynamic detail a direct axis; MonoDyGauBench warns that monocular dynamic GS is brittle and scene-dependent. ADAGS needs a narrower, failure-targeted claim.

W&B gives useful but incomplete evidence. The project has 126 finished runs. In the mechanism screen at 600k, `lora_route0` leads at 34.2596 PSNR; `lora_route0_dyn` is 33.7631; `scaffold_lora_route0_noreg` is 33.8534; `scaffold_lora_route0_reg` is 33.9445; `scaffold_lora_route0_dyn` is 33.1592. In the later eval-6000 cohort, naive flow is weak at 33.1590 and core-mask ramp is 32.9321, while render-gated flow recovers to 34.2057. Phase-2 LoRA lanes are competitive, with r16 at 34.1426 and r8 coefficient/basis at 34.1332. Dynamic diagnostic metrics are only available on a small eval-6000 subset, so no final paper claim should rest on global PSNR alone.

## Recommended Ideas

### 1. Reliability-Gated Rendered Flow For Dynamic Cores

- **Method**: Start from LoRA route0. Load cached masks/flows through `MotionPriorCache`. Split pixels into reliable dynamic core, uncertain boundary, and static anchor/background. Apply rendered-flow loss only in reliable dynamic core pixels; handle boundary and static zones with weaker or separate constraints.
- **Hypothesis**: Flow supervision helps dynamic sharpness only when gated away from occlusions, boundaries, and unreliable static leakage.
- **Minimum experiment**: Use existing W&B eval-6000 evidence to compare naive flow, core-mask ramp, render-gated flow, and phase-2 LoRA. Future minimal run: one controlled three-scene checkpoint-6000 eval with identical LoRA base, same masks, dynamic diagnostics, and render-gate ablation. Do not run now.
- **Expected outcome**: Success means render-gated flow improves dynamic-mask PSNR or sharpness proxies while matching route0 global PSNR and static quality; failure means dynamic diagnostics do not improve despite global PSNR recovery.
- **Novelty risk**: Medium-high. Flow guidance is crowded by MotionGS, SplatFlow, PaMoSplat, and Flow4DGS-SLAM. The novelty must be reliability gating plus diagnostics, not flow itself.
- **Feasibility risk**: Medium. The render-gated branch exists remotely, but masks/flows may be noisy or missing and dynamic metrics are sparse.
- **Nearest related work**: MotionGS, SplatFlow, PaMoSplat, Flow4DGS-SLAM, Shape of Motion.
- **Likely reviewer objection**: "This is just an optical-flow loss with masks; why is the gating principled or general?"
- **Evidence**: W&B eval-6000: naive flow 33.1590, core-mask ramp 32.9321, render-gated flow 34.2057 with dynamic-mask PSNR 23.1831. Code has `enable_rendered_flow`, `lambda_track_flow`, `compute_flow_loss`, and mask/flow loading.

### 2. Dynamic-Region Diagnostic Benchmark As The Paper Backbone

- **Method**: Standardize a small ADAGS diagnostic suite over the N3V cooking scenes. Report global PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS plus dynamic-mask PSNR, static-region PSNR, static ghost score, dynamic edge/detail magnitude, track-flow L1, routing entropy, expected static/dynamic points, realized point counts, and qualitative panels.
- **Hypothesis**: Global PSNR hides the actual ADAGS failure; dynamic-region diagnostics will separate stable route0, harmful broad flow, promising render-gated flow, and weak scaffold variants more clearly than aggregate PSNR.
- **Minimum experiment**: Existing W&B and wiki summaries already support the need. Future minimal run: eval-only diagnostic pass for the 15 mechanism-screen models using existing checkpoints and masks. Do not submit it now.
- **Expected outcome**: Success means the diagnostic table changes method ranking or reveals a route0-vs-flow tradeoff hidden by PSNR; failure means masks are too noisy and diagnostics do not rank methods consistently.
- **Novelty risk**: Medium-high. A benchmark-only story may look insufficient for a main method paper.
- **Feasibility risk**: Low-medium. Code and scripts exist, but W&B diagnostic coverage is incomplete.
- **Nearest related work**: MonoDyGauBench, AdaGaR, Multi4D, D4RT, MotionScale.
- **Likely reviewer objection**: "This is evaluation infrastructure, not a new reconstruction method."
- **Evidence**: Code logs dynamic/static metrics in validation and has `submit_mechanism_screen_diagnostic_eval_6000.sh`; W&B has 126 finished runs but dynamic diagnostics only on a small eval-6000 subset; wiki identifies G7 as high priority.

### 3. Route0-Coherent Plus Residual-Specialist Motion

- **Method**: Keep reversible LoRA route0 as the base motion. Add a small residual specialist only for high-error/high-motion dynamic regions, using a second LoRA phase or localized residual path. Diagnose whether route0 explains coherent motion while residuals explain local non-rigid or high-frequency failures.
- **Hypothesis**: Route0 is stable because it captures broad coherent motion; a residual specialist can improve localized dynamic detail without destabilizing static regions.
- **Minimum experiment**: Use existing W&B phase-2 LoRA lanes as signal. Future minimal run: compare route0, phase-2 LoRA, and render-gated residual on the same three scenes with dynamic diagnostics and equal realized point counts. Do not run now.
- **Expected outcome**: Success means residual-specialist lanes improve dynamic-mask PSNR/detail without lowering static PSNR or increasing static ghost score; failure means route0 already captures the available signal or residual capacity overfits.
- **Novelty risk**: High. RiGS, MoE-GS, MAPo, PaMoSplat, HiCoM, and continuous-motion bases already specialize motion. The contribution must be reversible route0/residual specialization plus empirical failure localization.
- **Feasibility risk**: Medium. Phase-2 LoRA exists in remote branches/W&B, but decomposition diagnostics are not yet broad enough.
- **Nearest related work**: RiGS, MoE-GS, MAPo, PaMoSplat, HiCoM, SE(3) B-spline continuous motion.
- **Likely reviewer objection**: "The coherent/residual interpretation is post hoc unless you prove the two paths specialize."
- **Evidence**: `lora_route0` is the stable baseline. W&B phase-2 r16 reaches 34.1426 and r8 coefficient/basis reaches 34.1332; not a clear win, but competitive enough to study with diagnostics.

### 4. Confidence-Aware Priors For Masks, Tracks, And Flow

- **Method**: Convert masks, residuals, flow consistency, and render agreement into a confidence weight. Apply dynamic ROI, static exclusion, and flow/track losses with this confidence instead of binary masks. Downweight occlusions, disocclusions, motion boundaries, and static/dynamic disagreement.
- **Hypothesis**: The main problem with priors is not their type but their reliability; confidence weighting will preserve route0 stability while letting priors help the parts of the sequence where they are trustworthy.
- **Minimum experiment**: Existing evidence is indirect: broad priors hurt, render-gated priors recover. Future minimal run: one route0 confidence-gating ablation with the same cached priors and no scaffold, then optionally repeat with residual specialist. Do not run now.
- **Expected outcome**: Success means confidence weighting improves dynamic metrics over binary dynamic ROI/static exclusion and broad flow; failure means confidence estimates are too correlated with easy pixels and avoid the hard dynamic regions.
- **Novelty risk**: Medium. USplat4D covers uncertainty-aware dynamic GS, so ADAGS must be specific about mask/flow/track reliability in cooking scenes.
- **Feasibility risk**: Medium-high. Confidence design is not implemented as a single model path and may need careful calibration.
- **Nearest related work**: USplat4D, Prior-Enhanced GS, Shape of Motion, SplatFlow.
- **Likely reviewer objection**: "This adds heuristic confidence weights without showing why they transfer beyond these scenes."
- **Evidence**: `MotionPriorCache` already handles masks and flows; W&B says naive flow is bad but render-gated flow is plausible; wiki G9 is open.

### 5. Dynamic Frequency/Detail Preservation Under Fixed Budget

- **Method**: Evaluate and optionally optimize dynamic-region high-frequency detail under matched realized point budgets. Use dynamic edge/detail metrics, local frequency residuals, dynamic point density, and visual crops to audit whether methods preserve moving-object detail rather than merely improving average color PSNR.
- **Hypothesis**: ADAGS failures are partly representation-frequency failures; method variants that look similar in global PSNR may differ in high-frequency dynamic detail.
- **Minimum experiment**: Existing W&B has dynamic edge metric hooks but sparse diagnostic coverage. Future minimal run: eval-only diagnostics on existing checkpoints plus one controlled budgeted follow-up only if diagnostics show a clear detail gap. Do not run now.
- **Expected outcome**: Success means the report can show dynamic-detail gains for render-gated/residual methods at equal point count; failure means edge/detail proxies are too noisy or not aligned with visual blur.
- **Novelty risk**: Medium-high. AdaGaR and Multi4D already frame frequency/detail strongly.
- **Feasibility risk**: Medium. Metrics are easy; making them convincing to reviewers is harder.
- **Nearest related work**: AdaGaR, Multi4D, SharpTimeGS, MAPo.
- **Likely reviewer objection**: "A proxy edge metric is not perceptual sharpness and can reward artifacts."
- **Evidence**: Code logs `test/dynamic_edge_magnitude`; W&B fixed-budget and mechanism screens prove global PSNR alone is insufficient; wiki G11 marks this as a high-priority gap.

### 6. Track-Anchored Scaffold Residual Motion

- **Method**: Keep LoRA route0 as the base. Use dense long-range track-flow caches to supervise rendered flow or scaffold-attached Gaussian motion. Let scaffold residuals move only where tracks and dynamic masks agree; monitor scaffold coefficient norms and attachment entropy.
- **Hypothesis**: Scaffold residual motion failed because it was unanchored; with long-range correspondence it can capture local coherent deviations that route0 smooths away.
- **Minimum experiment**: Existing W&B is negative for unanchored scaffold. Future minimal run: three-scene 600k comparison of route0, scaffold without flow, and scaffold with track-flow, using diagnostic metrics. Do not run now.
- **Expected outcome**: Success means scaffold improves dynamic metrics only when track-flow is active; failure means scaffold capacity remains unused or destabilizes route0.
- **Novelty risk**: High. MoSca and Prior-Enhanced GS already own much of the scaffold-plus-prior space.
- **Feasibility risk**: High. Track caches and occlusion masks may be the limiting factor, and current configs set `lambda_track_flow: 0.0`.
- **Nearest related work**: MoSca, Prior-Enhanced GS, Shape of Motion, MotionGS.
- **Likely reviewer objection**: "This is MoSca/Prior-Enhanced GS with a different base representation."
- **Evidence**: Code has scaffold nodes, KNN attachment, rendered flow, track-flow loading, and scaffold diagnostics. W&B mechanism screen is a stop sign against unanchored scaffold: scaffold variants trail route0.

## Eliminated Or Archived Ideas

### 7. Eliminated: Unanchored Scaffold Residual Motion

- **Method**: Add scaffold residual motion on top of LoRA route0, with KNN-style Gaussian attachment and smoothness/regularization losses, but without active track-flow or rendered-flow anchoring.
- **Hypothesis tested**: A scaffold residual path alone should capture local coherent deviations that route0 misses.
- **Minimum experiment**: Already represented by fixed-budget and mechanism-screen scaffold variants. No new pilot should be launched before track/correspondence anchoring is introduced.
- **Novelty risk**: Very high. MoSca and Prior-Enhanced GS already make scaffold-plus-prior methods central; an unanchored scaffold is weaker than that literature.
- **Feasibility risk**: Medium. The code runs, but the residual can remain unused or hurt optimization.
- **Nearest related work**: MoSca, Prior-Enhanced GS, Shape of Motion.
- **Likely reviewer objection**: "This is a less supervised scaffold than prior work, and it does not beat your own route0 baseline."
- **Evidence**: Fixed-budget scaffold is mixed; mechanism-screen scaffold variants are below route0; current configs keep `lambda_track_flow: 0.0`.
- **Decision**: Archive as a paper lead. Revisit only as Idea 6 with active long-range correspondence and diagnostics.

### 8. Eliminated: Naive Broad Flow Supervision

- **Method**: Apply a flow loss broadly from cached file masks/flows without sufficiently separating reliable dynamic cores, boundaries, occlusions, and static anchors.
- **Hypothesis tested**: Direct flow supervision should sharpen dynamic regions.
- **Minimum experiment**: Existing eval-6000 flow lanes are enough to reject broad flow as the lead. Future runs should only test reliability-gated variants.
- **Novelty risk**: Very high. Plain flow guidance is already crowded by MotionGS, SplatFlow, PaMoSplat, and Flow4DGS-SLAM.
- **Feasibility risk**: Medium-high. Flow noise and occlusion errors are exactly where cooking-scene dynamics are hardest.
- **Nearest related work**: MotionGS, SplatFlow, PaMoSplat, Flow4DGS-SLAM.
- **Likely reviewer objection**: "Optical flow losses are known; the paper needs a reason this one is robust."
- **Evidence**: W&B eval-6000 naive flow is 33.1590 PSNR and core-mask ramp is 32.9321, both below render-gated flow at 34.2057.
- **Decision**: Kill as a lead. Preserve only as the negative control for Idea 1.

### 9. Eliminated: Hard Static Conversion

- **Method**: Irreversibly convert Gaussians between static and dynamic sets using thresholded staticness/dynamicness rules.
- **Hypothesis tested**: Hard conversion should prevent static/dynamic leakage by enforcing a discrete representation split.
- **Minimum experiment**: Existing archive and branch history are sufficient; no new run is justified except as a narrow ablation.
- **Novelty risk**: High. Static/dynamic decomposition is heavily occupied by SWinGS, SplatFlow, 4DGS-SLAM, SharpTimeGS, and Hybrid 3D-4DGS.
- **Feasibility risk**: High. Early irreversible mistakes and brittle thresholds can damage both motion and static background.
- **Nearest related work**: SWinGS, SplatFlow, Hybrid 3D-4DGS, 4DGS-SLAM.
- **Likely reviewer objection**: "The method relies on brittle thresholding and is less elegant than reversible routing."
- **Evidence**: Existing wiki archive marks this negative; current code direction moved to opacity-conservative reversible routing.
- **Decision**: Keep only as an ablation baseline.

### 10. Eliminated/Conditional: Blur Curriculum And Early Part-Basis As Tried

- **Method**: Blur training targets early, or use early learned part-basis motion without route0 initialization and strong external priors.
- **Hypothesis tested**: Coarse-to-fine smoothing or unsupervised part bases should reduce motion blur.
- **Minimum experiment**: Existing notes and wiki archive are enough for the tried forms. Do not revive either as a main method.
- **Novelty risk**: High for blur curriculum; medium-high for part-basis unless reformulated with reversible routing and external tracks.
- **Feasibility risk**: Low for blur but low upside; medium-high for part-basis because assignments can be unstable.
- **Nearest related work**: MAPo, PaMoSplat, MoE-GS, HiCoM for the stronger part-aware version; no-blur LoRA route0 for the local baseline.
- **Likely reviewer objection**: "Blur curriculum attacks the symptom rather than the motion model; early part bases are not controlled enough to support a new claim."
- **Evidence**: Wiki archive records no-blur LoRA route0 stronger than blur variants, and part-basis below route0 in PSNR/LPIPS.
- **Decision**: Do not revive blur as a lead. Revisit part-aware motion only under Idea 3 with route0 initialization and external mask/track clusters.

## Suggested Paper Shapes

### A. Safest

**Title shape**: Reliability-Gated Priors for Dynamic-Region Sharpness in Adaptive Gaussian Splatting.

Core paper: Ideas 1 + 2. Treat diagnostics as the backbone and render-gated flow/confidence gating as the method. This is the best balance of evidence, feasibility, and reviewer defensibility.

### B. Higher Novelty

**Title shape**: Reversible Coherent-Residual Motion Specialization for Dynamic Gaussian Splatting.

Core paper: Ideas 2 + 3 + 5. This is stronger if diagnostics prove residual/phase-2 capacity specializes on dynamic failures and does not just add parameters.

### C. Risky Backup

**Title shape**: Correspondence-Anchored Scaffold Residuals for Cooking-Scene Dynamic Gaussian Splatting.

Core paper: Idea 6. Only pursue if future track-flow diagnostics show a clear positive signal; otherwise reviewers will compare it unfavorably to MoSca and Prior-Enhanced GS.

# 4C4D: 4 Camera 4D Gaussian Splatting

## One-line thesis

Spatial-temporal visibility detection lets a learned opacity-decay function focus sparse-view 4DGS gradients on geometrically useful visible primitives.

## Problem / Gap

With as few as four cameras, 4DGS can overfit appearance while learning weak geometry; uniform opacity decay also perturbs primitives that receive no current rendering gradient.

## Method

4C4D learns a Neural Decaying Function from Gaussian position, opacity, and rotation. A view-intersection test and each Gaussian's temporal span identify the currently active subset; learned decay is applied to that subset while other primitives receive a small constant decay.

## Key Results

The CVPR 2026 paper reports improved PSNR, DSSIM, and LPIPS on sparse-view dynamic benchmarks, including Neural3DV, and ablates both neural decay and visibility detection.

## Assumptions

Visibility is approximated through view/frustum intersection and temporal-span membership of existing 4D Gaussians. Training remains photometric and per-scene.

## Limitations / Failure Modes

The visibility test is not a calibrated front/back z-order or reveal-state estimator. The mechanism modulates opacity learning on existing primitives and does not explicitly create, preserve, or reassign hidden-surface capacity.

## Reusable Ingredients

Separate policies for currently active and inactive primitives, sparse-camera evaluation, and an explicit visibility-conditioned optimization ablation.

## Open Questions

Does explicit multiview depth order outperform center/frustum and temporal-span activity when surfaces occlude one another?

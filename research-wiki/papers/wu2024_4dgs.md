# 4D Gaussian Splatting for Real-Time Dynamic Scene Rendering

**Paper:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.08528
**Code:** https://github.com/hustvl/4DGaussians
**Base method:** 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al. 2023) + K-Planes / HexPlane (Fridovich-Keil et al. 2023) spatial-temporal feature grids

## One-line thesis

A single set of canonical 3D Gaussians plus a shared multi-resolution HexPlane voxel encoder and a lightweight multi-head MLP decoder predict per-timestep position/rotation/scale deltas for every Gaussian, replacing per-frame independent 3DGS optimization with one compact, jointly-trained deformation field that renders in real time.

## Problem / Gap

Applying 3D Gaussian Splatting independently to each frame of a dynamic sequence scales memory and storage linearly with frame count (O(t·N)) and does not share structure across time, while NeRF-based dynamic methods are accurate but too slow for real-time playback. The paper targets a representation that stays compact (one canonical Gaussian set) and fast (explicit rasterization) while still capturing per-Gaussian motion over time.

## Method

Gaussians are initialized once (as in static 3DGS) from a SfM point cloud, then a 3,000-iteration warm-up trains them as if the scene were static to obtain a stable canonical set. After warm-up, each canonical Gaussian's center and timestamp query a spatial-temporal structure encoder built from six multi-resolution 2D voxel planes (the three spatial planes and three space-time planes of HexPlane/K-Planes); the interpolated multi-resolution features from all six planes are fused and passed through a small MLP into a shared latent feature. Three separate small MLP heads decode this latent into position, rotation, and scaling deltas, which are added to the canonical Gaussian to produce the deformed Gaussian set for that timestamp; color/opacity are not deformed. The deformed Gaussians are rasterized with the standard differentiable 3DGS renderer and supervised with an L1 photometric loss plus a grid total-variation regularizer on the voxel planes.

## Assumptions

Assumes multi-view (or dense monocular, e.g. D-NeRF-style) calibrated captures with known camera poses and a COLMAP-derived initial point cloud; treats scene motion as smooth, bounded per-Gaussian deformation from a single canonical geometry rather than topology change or object appearance/disappearance.

## Limitations / Failure Modes

Struggles with large/fast motion, where the deformation MLP over-smooths and produces blur on sports-style datasets. Under sparse monocular input it overfits the training views and fails on novel-view synthesis despite low training error, and it cannot reliably disentangle camera motion from object motion in monocular settings without extra supervision. Deformation-field queries become computationally prohibitive at very large (urban-scale) Gaussian counts, and inaccurate camera poses degrade convergence; the authors note these require additional priors such as depth or optical flow.

## Reusable Ingredients

- **HexPlane/K-Planes multi-resolution voxel encoder** — decomposes 4D space-time into 6 factored 2D planes for compact, fast spatio-temporal feature lookup, reusable anywhere a per-point space-time feature is needed.
- **3,000-iteration static warm-up before deformation training** — stabilizes canonical geometry before large deformation gradients are introduced; avoids the network fighting simultaneous geometry and motion optimization.
- **Multi-head delta decoding (position/rotation/scale as separate small MLPs off one shared latent)** — cheap way to add several independent output modalities without a large shared decoder.
- **Grid total-variation regularization on voxel-plane features** — smooths the learned feature grid to suppress high-frequency artifacts.

### Deep Dive

#### Core Novelty
Rather than a single MLP taking raw (x, t) as in prior deformable-GS works, 4DGS routes each Gaussian's (position, time) through a factored HexPlane voxel grid to get a spatio-temporal feature, then decodes that feature with separate lightweight heads per Gaussian attribute. The key insight is that the explicit multi-resolution plane factorization gives the network enough spatial-temporal inductive bias to learn deformation quickly and compactly, while keeping the query cost low enough for real-time rendering — replacing "one Gaussian set per frame" with "one canonical Gaussian set + one shared field."

#### Mathematical Formulation
Rendering: $\hat{I} = \mathcal{S}(M, \mathcal{G}')$ where $\mathcal{G}' = \Delta\mathcal{G} + \mathcal{G}$ and $\Delta\mathcal{G} = \mathcal{F}(\mathcal{G}, t)$ — the deformation network $\mathcal{F}$ maps the canonical Gaussian set and a query time $t$ to a delta, evaluated once per rendered frame before rasterization.

Each canonical Gaussian carries position $\mathcal{X} \in \mathbb{R}^3$, SH color coefficients $\mathcal{C} \in \mathbb{R}^k$, opacity $\alpha$, rotation quaternion $r \in \mathbb{R}^4$, and scale $s \in \mathbb{R}^3$.

Spatial-temporal encoder $\mathcal{H}$ (per-Gaussian, before rasterization): for each of the 6 plane pairs $(i,j) \in \{(x,y),(x,z),(y,z),(x,t),(y,t),(z,t)\}$, bilinearly interpolate multi-resolution plane features and combine:
$$f_h = \bigcup_l \prod \text{interp}(R_l(i,j)), \quad R_l(i,j) \in \mathbb{R}^{h \times lN_i \times lN_j}$$
where $h$ is the feature hidden dimension, $N$ the base grid resolution, and $l$ the upsampling scale. A small merge MLP $\phi_d$ fuses the concatenated plane features into a single latent: $f_d = \phi_d(f_h)$.

Multi-head deformation decoder (per-Gaussian, before rasterization): three independent small MLPs map the shared latent to per-attribute deltas — $\Delta\mathcal{X} = \phi_x(f_d)$, $\Delta r = \phi_r(f_d)$, $\Delta s = \phi_s(f_d)$ — applied additively to give the deformed Gaussian:
$$(\mathcal{X}', r', s') = (\mathcal{X} + \Delta\mathcal{X},\ r + \Delta r,\ s + \Delta s), \quad \mathcal{G}' = \{\mathcal{X}', s', r', \sigma, \mathcal{C}\}$$
Color and opacity are left undeformed.

Loss (after rendering): $\mathcal{L} = |\hat{I} - I| + \mathcal{L}_{tv}$, an L1 photometric term plus a total-variation regularizer on the voxel-plane grid features.

#### Algorithm / Pipeline Changes
1. Initialize a canonical 3D Gaussian set from a COLMAP SfM point cloud, as in static 3DGS.
2. Warm-up: optimize the canonical Gaussians alone for 3,000 iterations using standard static 3DGS rendering/loss ($\hat{I} = \mathcal{S}(M, \mathcal{G})$), with no deformation network active, to stabilize geometry before deformation gradients are introduced.
3. After warm-up, for every training/rendering step: query the 6-plane HexPlane encoder $\mathcal{H}$ at each Gaussian's canonical position and the current timestamp, interpolate and fuse the 6 plane features, and pass through merge MLP $\phi_d$ to get shared latent $f_d$.
4. Decode $f_d$ with three parallel small MLP heads ($\phi_x, \phi_r, \phi_s$) to get $(\Delta\mathcal{X}, \Delta r, \Delta s)$; add to the canonical Gaussian's $(\mathcal{X}, r, s)$ to form the deformed set $\mathcal{G}'$ for that timestamp. This step replaces per-frame independent Gaussian optimization.
5. Rasterize $\mathcal{G}'$ with the unmodified differentiable 3DGS renderer $\mathcal{S}$ to produce $\hat{I}$.
6. Backpropagate the L1 + grid-TV loss through the decoder heads, merge MLP, and voxel-plane parameters (and canonical Gaussian parameters); Gaussian densification/growth is stopped at iteration 15,000 and pruning runs every 8,000 iterations.

#### Key Hyperparameters & Design Choices
- HexPlane base grid resolution: 64 (increased for higher-detail datasets, e.g. hidden dim raised to 128 for HyperNeRF).
- Upsampling scales: 2, 4.
- Encoder (voxel-plane) learning rate: $1.6\times10^{-3} \to 1.6\times10^{-4}$ (decayed).
- Decoder (MLP heads) learning rate: $1.6\times10^{-4} \to 1.6\times10^{-5}$ (decayed).
- Batch size: 1.
- Warm-up (static-only) phase: 3,000 iterations.
- Total iterations: 20,000 (synthetic/D-NeRF-style), 14,000 (Neu3D/N3V-style).
- Gaussian densification stopped at iteration 15,000; pruning interval 8,000 iterations.
- Dense initial point cloud downsampled to <100k points for Neu3D-style scenes (repo README states <40k for some configs).
- MLP layer counts/widths for $\phi_d, \phi_x, \phi_r, \phi_s$: Not specified in paper.
- Loss weight for $\mathcal{L}_{tv}$: Not specified in paper.

#### Ablation Summary (synthetic dataset, full method = 34.05 dB PSNR)
- **w/o HexPlane (encoder removed): 27.05 dB, −6.9 dB — single most impactful component.**
- w/o position deformation head $\phi_x$: 26.67 dB, −7.38 dB (comparable magnitude to removing HexPlane entirely, and the largest single-head ablation).
- w/o static warm-up initialization: 31.91 dB, −2.14 dB (also ~2x slower to converge: 7.5 min vs 8 min at lower quality).
- w/o rotation deformation head $\phi_r$: 33.08 dB, −0.97 dB.
- w/o scaling deformation head $\phi_s$: 33.02 dB, −1.03 dB.

Position deformation and the HexPlane encoder itself are the two dominant contributors; rotation and scale deformation heads matter much less.

#### Implementation Reality
- **Framework:** PyTorch (pytorch=1.13.1+cu116), with custom CUDA extensions `depth-diff-gaussian-rasterization` (differentiable rasterizer) and `simple-knn` (nearest-neighbor ops), built on top of the original 3DGS codebase.
- **Key files:** HexPlane/K-Planes-derived spatial-temporal encoder and deformation network modules (README credits code borrowed from the HexPlane and K-Planes repos); `export_perframe_3DGS.py` for exporting per-frame deformed Gaussian sets.
- **Notable implementation details:** Training is split into explicit "coarse" and "fine" stages with separate checkpoints (`chkpnt_coarse_*.pth`, `chkpnt_fine_*.pth`), a distinction not fully surfaced in the paper's math. Post-publication repo updates report substantially faster training than originally published (8 min vs. 20 min on D-NeRF-style data; 30 min vs. 1 hour on HyperNeRF-style data), indicating the released code diverges from the paper's reported timings.

#### Failure Modes & Limitations
Deformation network blurs fast/large motion (shown on sports-style datasets). Sparse monocular capture causes overfitting to training views with failed novel-view synthesis despite good training-view quality. Monocular settings cannot reliably separate camera motion from object motion without extra supervision. Deformation-field queries do not scale well to urban-scale Gaussian counts, and inaccurate camera poses degrade convergence; the authors state these issues need additional priors (e.g., depth, optical flow) to resolve.

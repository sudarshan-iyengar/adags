## Claims independently re-verified before acceptance

Four load-bearing objections were checked numerically rather than taken on
trust. All four hold, and three of them overturn the v0.1 design.

1. **The plateau is a gradient dead zone.** Inside `[l,r]` the proposed
   `relu(d)` form has `da/dl = da/dr = da/dsigma = 0` exactly (verified to
   0.000e+00). Absence frames falling inside a broad initial support therefore
   supply **no endpoint gradient that could carve a gap**. v0.1 preserved the
   shipped gate's dead zone instead of removing it.
2. **Noisy-OR does not distribute gradient to all intervals.**
   `dA/da_k = prod_{j!=k}(1 - a_j)`, so if any other interval sits on its
   plateau (`a_j = 1`) interval *k* receives **exactly zero** gradient
   (verified 0.000e+00). The v0.1 comparison table asserted the opposite.
3. **K=2 noisy-OR does not nest K=1.** `A = a1 + a2 - a1*a2`; duplicating an
   interval gives `2a - a^2`, not `a` (0.9 becomes 0.99). So the v0.1 "nesting"
   claim was false, and with it the licence to read A2 underperformance as
   "optimisation failure, not capacity failure". `max` **is** idempotent and
   does nest.
4. **The reproduction gap was reported as best-of-six.** Published 33.7329;
   our best 33.1426 gives 0.5903 dB, our **mean 32.6731 gives 1.0598 dB**. The
   0.59 dB figure was quoted as the headline throughout the v0.1 plan, the
   brief and the progress reports. The mean gap is the honest number.

## The constructive alternative, also verified

The reviewer proposed replacing K exchangeable intervals with an **ordered
outer lifespan minus one learned absence notch**, boundaries generated by
cumulative `softplus` widths, edges as sigmoid products, and a gate `q` with an
exact-zero state. Checked numerically:

* interior absence frames **do** produce nonzero gradient on the notch
  boundaries (`dp/db1 = +3.5e-02` at t=45 of a [40,60] notch) — the dead zone
  of objection 1 is gone;
* `q = 0` reproduces the single-support model **exactly** (equality at every
  probe), so the capacity control becomes a one-scalar change on identical
  tensors rather than a projection whose cleanliness must be argued;
* cumulative softplus boundaries are monotone by construction, so ordering,
  permutation symmetry and optimiser-state permutation all disappear.

## The other accepted findings

* **`dominent_idxs` is not identity.** It is the single Gaussian maximising
  `alpha_i * T_i` per pixel; all non-winning contribution is discarded, a
  one-pixel lineage counts equally with a thousand-pixel one, and a tiny
  opacity change can flip the winner with no image change.
* **A lineage column cannot establish "the same primitive"** — split destroys
  the parent, clone creates descendants, promotion deletes the donor row. An
  inherited id means *same ancestral family*. Track `instance_id`,
  `root_lineage_id`, `parent_id`, birth/death iteration and operation, and
  report exact-instance survival separately from descendant survival.
* **"Created during the absence" is ill-defined** — cameras and timestamps are
  shuffled per epoch, so optimizer iteration is not scene chronology.
* **P1's 5% rule can pass entirely on off-event primitives.** Replace with a
  causal, event-specific non-vacuity test: disable the notch at fixed trained
  parameters and require a registered event-window change exceeding a
  repeat-render null band.
* **P2 is circular** (it is already a weak C2 success) and should be deleted.
* **A4 is not a clean capacity control** as specified: contiguous noisy-OR
  tails differ from K=1, the projection changes optimiser geometry, and K can
  change split/clone/prune counts and final primitive totals.
* **A design contradiction:** the six existing A0 runs were trained on the
  ordinary reproduction scene, **not** on the edited fixture, so they cannot
  satisfy P4 nor estimate fixture-trained event variance. A0 must be re-run on
  the fixture under the integrated commit.
* **Novelty is materially worse than v0.1 assumed.** Verified to exist by
  fetching the abstracts rather than accepting the citation:
  **Flow4DGS-SLAM** (arXiv 2604.22339) models *"the temporal opacity and
  rotation using a Gaussian Mixture Model"*; **TRiGS** (arXiv 2604.00538)
  frames *"primitives repeatedly eliminated and regenerated"* and
  *"eliminates the long-term temporal identity of objects"* as its problem.
  With PersistGS already owning object permanence under all-camera occlusion,
  a union of two temporal lobes is not novelty by itself.
* **What is undersold:** the auxiliary flow VJP already carries an
  `alpha_i*T_i`-weighted per-primitive accumulator. Injecting
  `object_mask x acc` at evaluation yields blend-aware contribution-mass
  attribution with **no trainable parameters and no CUDA change** — a far
  better identity instrument than dominant-index, and the closest analogue in
  this substrate to Multi4D's reuse of SH channels.
* **The trajectory may matter more than the gate.** Nothing constrains the
  pose through an unsupervised gap, and `motion_reg` compares the first
  keyframe against every later one rather than enforcing adjacent-keyframe
  smoothness.

# A. Technical soundness — Q1–Q4

## 1. The proposed per-interval distance is not complete

For correctly ordered endpoints \(l<r\),

\[
d(t)=\max(l-t,t-r),\qquad
a(t)=\exp[-\operatorname{ReLU}(d)^2/\sigma^2]
\]

is continuous and once differentiable at the interval edges. The square kills the ReLU derivative discontinuity: the first derivative approaches zero from outside and is zero inside. It is not twice differentiable, but that alone is not serious.

The serious problems are:

1. **The absence cannot carve a gap out of an existing plateau.**  
   For every sample strictly inside \([l,r]\),

   \[
   \frac{\partial a}{\partial l}
   =
   \frac{\partial a}{\partial r}
   =
   \frac{\partial a}{\partial \sigma}
   =0.
   \]

   Thus, if K=2 is born as two duplicated or broadly overlapping intervals, negative frames inside the authored absence provide no direct endpoint gradient that can split the support. The proposal preserves the shipped gate’s plateau dead zone rather than solving it. Photometric loss is the relevant supervision in [train.py:143–170](/D:/adags/refine-logs/review-2026-09-21/src/ex4dgs/train.py:143).

2. **Endpoint order is not enforced.**  
   Sorting intervals by `c0_k` does not enforce `c0_k <= c1_k`. If an individual interval crosses, `max(c0-t,t-c1)` becomes a positive V-shaped valley with a nondifferentiable midpoint—not an interval.

3. **Collapsed intervals can be absorbing.**  
   At \(l=r=t\), the boundary gradient is exactly zero. The existing degeneracy pruning is itself suspect because the result of a `logical_or` is discarded at [c_gaussian_model.py:1074–1083](/D:/adags/refine-logs/review-2026-09-21/src/ex4dgs/scene/c_gaussian_model.py:1074).

4. **“Gradient everywhere” is false downstream.**  
   Gaussian tails can underflow; moreover, the exact Ex4DGS rasterizer drops per-pixel alpha below approximately \(1/255\) at `submodules/diff_gaussian_rasterization_df/cuda_rasterizer/forward.cu:379–380`. Multi4D is even harder: it returns immediately below temporal marginal 0.05 at [forward.cu:281–285](/D:/adags/refine-logs/review-2026-09-21/src/multi4d/cuda_rasterizer/forward.cu:281) and [backward.cu:688–692](/D:/adags/refine-logs/review-2026-09-21/src/multi4d/cuda_rasterizer/backward.cu:688).

5. **The scale parameterization is underspecified.**  
   The current gate converts a raw variance through `exp(raw)+floor` at [interpolations.py:55–61](/D:/adags/refine-logs/review-2026-09-21/src/ex4dgs/utils/interpolations.py:55). Literal learned \(\sigma\) would permit zero or negative scales.

6. **The implementation surface is incomplete.**  
   `expand_duration`, called during progressive growth, still hard-indexes the two-element layout at [c_gaussian_model.py:1275–1279](/D:/adags/refine-logs/review-2026-09-21/src/ex4dgs/scene/c_gaussian_model.py:1275) and [train.py:261–267](/D:/adags/refine-logs/review-2026-09-21/src/ex4dgs/train.py:261). The proposed `[N,K,2,1]` shape also conflicts with PLY attribute enumeration versus flattening at [c_gaussian_model.py:503–545](/D:/adags/refine-logs/review-2026-09-21/src/ex4dgs/scene/c_gaussian_model.py:503).

7. **No K=2 birth policy is specified.**  
   Dynamic primitives are created by static-to-dynamic promotion, whose current initializer constructs exactly one two-edge interval at [c_gaussian_model.py:1178–1187](/D:/adags/refine-logs/review-2026-09-21/src/ex4dgs/scene/c_gaussian_model.py:1178). Initialization is load-bearing because duplicated intervals can remain symmetry-locked and plateau-dead.

So: the seven-line K=1 diagnosis is valid, but the replacement is not ready to implement as written.

## 2. Noisy-OR is the wrong primary operator here

For

\[
A=1-\prod_j(1-a_j),
\qquad
\frac{\partial A}{\partial a_k}
=\prod_{j\neq k}(1-a_j).
\]

This directly contradicts the plan’s statement that noisy-OR gives gradient to all intervals at [plan:95–108](/D:/adags/research-wiki/operations/ex4dgs-multi-interval-plan-2026-09-20.md:95):

- If any other interval is on its exact plateau, \(a_j=1\), interval \(k\) receives exactly zero union gradient.
- On interval \(k\)’s own plateau, \(da_k/d\theta_k=0\).
- Near saturation, gradients are strongly attenuated.
- Overlapping intervals therefore make the dead-gradient problem worse, not better.

More damaging: **K=2 noisy-OR does not strictly contain K=1**, contrary to [plan:82–86](/D:/adags/research-wiki/operations/ex4dgs-multi-interval-plan-2026-09-20.md:82). With two finite Gaussian-tailed intervals,

\[
A=a_1+a_2-a_1a_2.
\]

Recovering \(A=a_1\) requires \(a_2(1-a_1)=0\) everywhere. There is no exact “interval off” state, and duplicating an interval gives \(2a-a^2\), not \(a\). Therefore A2 underperformance cannot automatically be classified as “optimization failure.”

The prediction about `max` is only conditionally right: an interval that is never the argmax gets no gradient. But with ordered, non-overlapping initialization, each interval owns its temporal region. `max` is idempotent, preserves an exact K=1 submodel, and corresponds more closely to set union.

**Recommendation:** if retaining K intervals, use ordered disjoint intervals with `max`, and treat noisy-OR as an ablation. Better still, replace the K-interval representation as described under Q4.

## 3. Post-step sorting is unsafe as specified

Sorting entire interval tuples is functionally a relabelling, so the rendered symmetric union need not jump. But “cheap sort, no gradient issue” at [plan:110–121](/D:/adags/research-wiki/operations/ex4dgs-multi-interval-plan-2026-09-20.md:110) is incomplete.

A correct projection must permute together:

- starts and ends;
- left/right scales;
- activity variables;
- interval-specific statistics;
- RAdam `exp_avg` and `exp_avg_sq`.

The centers and variances are separate optimizer groups at [c_gaussian_model.py:430–445](/D:/adags/refine-logs/review-2026-09-21/src/ex4dgs/scene/c_gaussian_model.py:430). Sorting parameter values without the optimizer states silently gives one interval another interval’s momentum.

At equal starts, canonical tuple ordering can also be discontinuous even if the rendered union is invariant.

**Use cumulative positive widths and gaps instead of post-step sorting.**

## 4. A fundamentally better parameterization

For the stated problem—present, one complete absence, then present—I would not use exchangeable K intervals or an occupancy MLP. I would use an **ordered outer lifespan minus one learned absence notch**:

\[
b_0 < b_1 \le b_2 < b_3,
\]

with the boundaries generated by cumulative `softplus` widths/gaps, and

\[
B_\tau(t;l,r)
=\sigma((t-l)/\tau)\sigma((r-t)/\tau),
\]

\[
p(t)=B_\tau(t;b_0,b_3)\,[1-qB_\tau(t;b_1,b_2)].
\]

Give \(q\) an exact-zero state.

This provides:

- \(q=0\): exact single-support control;
- \(q=1\): present–absent–present;
- no permutation symmetry;
- no crossings or overlap degeneracy;
- no duplicated-interval initialization;
- gradient from interior absence frames to the notch boundaries;
- a matched-parameter control obtained by fixing \(q=0\).

If sharp edges are ultimately needed, anneal \(\tau\) after the notch has been discovered. For multiple gaps, use several ordered sparse notches. A free occupancy MLP would weaken identifiability, capacity matching, and interpretability while permitting arbitrary flicker.

K=2 remains defensible only as a narrow engineering representation after adding ordered positive widths/gaps, an exact null interval, a non-symmetric initializer, and explicit gap/overlap handling.

# B. Scientific rigor — Q5–Q9

## 5. A0–A5 are insufficient; A4 is not a clean capacity control

A4 is useful, but the claim that A2 over A4 is attributable “to multi-interval presence and nothing else” at [plan:160–166](/D:/adags/research-wiki/operations/ex4dgs-multi-interval-plan-2026-09-20.md:160) is false.

With two contiguous noisy-OR intervals:

- overlapping tails create a different shoulder from K=1;
- internal endpoints/scales can be unidentifiable;
- the contiguity projection changes optimizer geometry;
- nominal parameter equality does not imply equal effective capacity;
- K can change split, clone, prune, and surviving primitive counts.

The dynamic split/clone paths perturb temporal centers and reset variances at [c_gaussian_model.py:940–945](/D:/adags/refine-logs/review-2026-09-21/src/ex4dgs/scene/c_gaussian_model.py:940) and [c_gaussian_model.py:1002–1007](/D:/adags/refine-logs/review-2026-09-21/src/ex4dgs/scene/c_gaussian_model.py:1002). Those topology differences must be measured.

Add:

- **A6: corrected K=1 with matched final active-primitive or densification budget.** This directly tests the alternative that K=1 simply respawns more primitives.
- **An oracle-initialized K=2 diagnostic.** This distinguishes representation failure from gap-discovery failure.
- Final counts and contribution-weighted births/splits/prunes for every arm.

There is also a design contradiction: the six existing A0 runs were trained on the ordinary reproduction scene, not each edited fixture. They cannot satisfy P4’s same-data requirement or estimate fixture-trained event variance. A0 must be rerun on the fixture under the integrated implementation commit.

A3 and A5 are secondary. Do not fund them before A2 beats A1, A4, and A6.

## 6. Preconditions P1–P4 need replacement

**P1: reject the global 5% rule.** It can pass entirely on irrelevant off-event primitives, while the event occupies a tiny fraction of pixel-time.

Use a causal, event-specific non-vacuity test:

1. At fixed trained parameters, disable the second support/notch.
2. Require a change in the registered event-window render or object-mask contribution exceeding both:
   - the 99th percentile of repeat-render/permuted-mask null variation; and
   - the frozen smallest claim-relevant effect.
3. Require the exercised lineages to carry measurable object-mask contribution before and after, across multiple cameras.
4. Require their contribution during the gap to fall below the renderer’s effective visibility threshold.

This tests whether the mechanism actually affected the event, not how many parameter vectors happen to have separated endpoints.

**P2 is circular and should be deleted.** Requiring an interval-straddling primitive to dominate before and after is already a weak C2 success. If there is no overlap, C2 should be zero—not “untestable.” Testability requires valid masks and observable object signal on both sides.

**P3 must hold for 100% of relevant contribution mass.** Allowing 1% of A4 primitives to have a gap is fatal if that 1% explains the object. Enforce the constraint by construction and assert it numerically for every primitive.

**P4 needs a common manifest plus declared arm overlay**, not identical config hashes. Freeze:

- edited-data and mask hashes;
- camera split;
- point cloud;
- paired seeds;
- environment and CUDA-extension hash;
- evaluator and rendering flags;
- integrated code commit;
- arm-specific overlay.

Add **P5: validate the attribution/lineage instrument** on a synthetic overlapping-splat scene and mutation unit tests.

## 7. C2 does not measure the claimed identity

On the exact Ex4DGS checkout, `dominent_idxs` is the one Gaussian maximizing \(\alpha_iT_i\) per pixel (`submodules/diff_gaussian_rasterization_df/cuda_rasterizer/forward.cu:411–415`). It is exposed at [gaussian_renderer/__init__.py:100–121](/D:/adags/refine-logs/review-2026-09-21/src/ex4dgs/gaussian_renderer/__init__.py:100) but not otherwise used.

That is not adequate attribution:

- pixels are blends, but all non-winning contribution is discarded;
- tiny opacity/depth changes can flip the winner without meaningful image change;
- set recall gives a one-pixel lineage equal status to a thousand-pixel lineage;
- background and occluder pixels contaminate a box-shaped ROI;
- `-1` pixels must be excluded;
- one camera cannot establish multiview identity.

More fundamentally, `_lineage` cannot establish “the same primitive”:

- split creates children and destroys the parent at [c_gaussian_model.py:874–964](/D:/adags/refine-logs/review-2026-09-21/src/ex4dgs/scene/c_gaussian_model.py:874);
- clone creates descendants at [c_gaussian_model.py:966–1017](/D:/adags/refine-logs/review-2026-09-21/src/ex4dgs/scene/c_gaussian_model.py:966);
- static-to-dynamic promotion creates a new dynamic row and deletes the donor at [c_gaussian_model.py:1147–1227](/D:/adags/refine-logs/review-2026-09-21/src/ex4dgs/scene/c_gaussian_model.py:1147).

An inherited lineage means **same ancestral family**, not same primitive. Also, “created during the absence” is ill-defined because training cameras/timestamps are shuffled at [train.py:117–125](/D:/adags/refine-logs/review-2026-09-21/src/ex4dgs/train.py:117); optimizer iteration is not scene chronology.

Track immutable:

- `instance_id`;
- `root_lineage_id`;
- `parent_id`;
- birth iteration and operation;
- death iteration and operation.

Then separately report exact-instance survival, descendant survival, and new-root mass.

The better attribution already exists in the code. The renderer passes a dummy three-channel per-Gaussian `flow` tensor at [gaussian_renderer/__init__.py:62–109](/D:/adags/refine-logs/review-2026-09-21/src/ex4dgs/gaussian_renderer/__init__.py:62), and training injects `[acc, L1, SSIM]` through its VJP at [train.py:147–153](/D:/adags/refine-logs/review-2026-09-21/src/ex4dgs/train.py:147). The CUDA backward accumulates \(\alpha_iT_i\)-weighted gradients into each primitive.

At evaluation, inject `object_mask × acc` and compute

\[
W_{\ell,w}
=\sum_{c,t,p\in\text{object mask}}
\alpha_{\ell,p}T_{\ell,p}.
\]

Report:

- pre/post lineage-distribution overlap;
- retained pre-gap contribution mass;
- post-return mass from exact instances, descendants, and new roots;
- a causal ablation of the pre-gap cohort;
- an operational colour-edit or semantic-label transfer across the gap.

Keep dominant-index maps only as visualizations.

## 9. Authored absence does not license physical disappearance

Authored fixtures license only:

> On controlled counterfactual-removal fixtures, disconnected temporal support improves event rendering and/or ancestral-lineage reuse relative to this Ex4DGS reproduction.

They do not license natural disappearance, physical object permanence, or general occlusion. The earlier real-occlusion study found that the gate could have nothing appropriate to remove or restore and could remove the occluder instead at [query_pack.md:382–388](/D:/adags/research-wiki/query_pack.md:382). The prior review already rejected authored-to-physical equivalence at [query_pack.md:233–247](/D:/adags/research-wiki/query_pack.md:233).

The minimum physical proof-of-principle is:

- a calibrated synchronized multiview capture;
- the object physically leaves the shared reconstructed volume and later returns;
- all-view/free-space evidence that it is absent, not merely untracked;
- independently annotated same-object identity;
- changed-pose return;
- frozen object masks and held-out cameras;
- A1/A2/A4/A6 with C1 and contribution-weighted C2.

One event licenses only a proof-of-principle. A top-venue claim needs multiple objects, scenes, gap lengths, and at least two independently captured physical events.

# C. Contribution and top-venue sufficiency — Q10–Q12

## 10. Current contribution is insufficient

The plan is correct that the continuity fix, union, and sorting are mostly craft. In fact:

- the sorting scheme is incomplete;
- noisy-OR is mathematically ill-suited;
- dominant-index C2 is not a valid identity measurement.

The potentially novel contribution is narrower:

> A disconnected temporal-support representation that demonstrably preserves useful object state across genuine all-view absence, verified through contribution-weighted, mutation-aware, operational identity tests.

That is not yet demonstrated.

The 2026 novelty landscape also makes the current framing risky:

- [Flow4DGS-SLAM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.22339) already advertises a Gaussian-mixture model for temporal opacity and rotation.
- [SharpTimeGS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02989) uses learnable flat-top lifespan modulation.
- [TRiGS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00538) explicitly frames primitive regeneration and long-term temporal identity as its problem.
- [PersistGS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03479) directly targets object permanence during all-camera occlusion using physical trajectory supervision.

A union of two temporal lobes is therefore not enough novelty by itself. The paper needs a sharp differentiation from mixture opacity and lifespan methods, plus evidence that preserved identity has downstream value.

What is currently undersold is the **soft compositor-attribution instrument** and an operational edit/label-transfer test. That could be a useful technical contribution, though not a standalone top-venue paper.

## 11. Small contributions available in the code

Two credible capacity-reuse opportunities exist.

1. **Reuse the auxiliary flow VJP as a contribution probe.**  
   This gives blend-aware per-Gaussian ROI attribution with no trainable parameters and no CUDA modification. It is much more defensible than `dominent_idxs`.

2. **Reinterpret the four existing temporal scalars as four ordered change points.**  
   Ex4DGS already stores two centers plus two raw variances per dynamic Gaussian at [c_gaussian_model.py:83–86](/D:/adags/refine-logs/review-2026-09-21/src/ex4dgs/scene/c_gaussian_model.py:83). A capacity-neutral ablation could encode \(b_0,b_1,b_2,b_3\) and use global/fixed edge softness. This sacrifices learned left/right tail widths, so it is an ablation—not automatically the best model.

There is no safely unused SH channel: all SH tensors are returned as appearance features at [c_gaussian_model.py:337–353](/D:/adags/refine-logs/review-2026-09-21/src/ex4dgs/scene/c_gaussian_model.py:337). `_xyz_disp` is not spare; it drives static motion and static-to-dynamic selection at [c_gaussian_model.py:178–180](/D:/adags/refine-logs/review-2026-09-21/src/ex4dgs/scene/c_gaussian_model.py:178) and [c_gaussian_model.py:1153–1160](/D:/adags/refine-logs/review-2026-09-21/src/ex4dgs/scene/c_gaussian_model.py:1153).

The keyframe layout is more important than hiding another variable: the gate can reactivate a primitive, but it does not make its trajectory correct after an unsupervised gap. The current `motion_reg` compares the first keyframe against every later keyframe, not adjacent-keyframe smoothness, at [train.py:159–161](/D:/adags/refine-logs/review-2026-09-21/src/ex4dgs/train.py:159). A boundary/re-entry trajectory constraint may be more load-bearing than the gate itself.

## 12. Strongest argument to stop

The strongest rejection argument is:

> Gaussian identity is not identifiable and may have no user-visible value. The renderer can split, clone, prune, or respawn exchangeable basis functions while producing the same images. A copied lineage ID can manufacture “continuity” even though the original row was destroyed. Meanwhile, disconnected opacity does not constrain the trajectory through the gap. The method may preserve bookkeeping rather than an object.

The project already found that an STG-style temporal-opacity model rendered the authored gap **3.4–7.5 dB better** than the gated arm, although return performance was worse, at [query_pack.md:187–195](/D:/adags/research-wiki/query_pack.md:187). This means “render the empty gap better” is not the contribution. Only correct return and useful identity can carry the paper.

The cheapest decision sequence is:

1. **One A1 run plus an oracle render-time notch/cohort reactivation**: 1.2 GPU-hours.  
   If the known pre-gap cohort returns at the wrong geometry even under oracle support, the bottleneck is trajectory/representation, not interval learning. Stop.

2. If oracle headroom exists, run paired `n=3` on A0/A1/A2/A4/A6: 15 runs, **18 GPU-hours**.  
   Kill the direction unless A2 exercises the mechanism and beats A1, A4, and A6 on C1 plus operational C2.

That is the maximum investment justified before a real capture exists.
